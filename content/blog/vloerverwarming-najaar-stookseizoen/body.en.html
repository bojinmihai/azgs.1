Why autumn is a smart moment

Underfloor heating works best when the system can warm up gradually and evenly. That is why it is smart to check it before the real cold starts. In September or October there is usually still time to solve small issues without emergency pressure.

In homes around Woerden and Utrecht we often see the same pattern: the floor has hardly heated during summer, the thermostat is still on a summer schedule, and only during the first cold days does one room fail to warm up properly. Annoying, but often avoidable.

Autumn is also a good moment for planning. If you want underfloor heating installed in an existing home, it can often be combined with floor preparation, leveling and a new floor finish.

Practical timing

Check underfloor heating before you start heating daily. That leaves time to bleed, adjust or have an installer inspect the system.

What you can check yourself

You do not have to be an installer to do a first check. Walk through the main points calmly and note anything unusual.

  • Thermostat: check whether the schedule, temperature and mode still fit the new season.
  • Manifold: look for leakage, damp marks or unusual sounds around the manifold.
  • Zones: after warming up, feel whether all rooms respond fairly evenly.
  • Pump: listen whether the pump runs normally and does not hum or stick.
  • Water pressure: check whether the boiler or heating system pressure is within the normal range.

Pay special attention to differences between rooms. A living room that warms well while the kitchen or bathroom stays behind often points to poor flow in one zone or an incorrect control setting.

Signs something is not right

Not every issue is serious, but some signals deserve attention. Do not wait until it is really cold outside.

  • The floor is warm in some areas and cold in others.
  • One room clearly stays below the set temperature.
  • You hear bubbling or ticking sounds in the pipes.
  • The manifold or pump feels extremely hot.
  • Energy use rises while comfort does not improve.

Common causes include air in the system, wrong settings, insufficient flow or a floor finish that blocks too much heat. Sometimes the cause is structural: too little insulation under the floor or a floor build-up that does not match underfloor heating well.

Underfloor heating during renovation

If you are renovating, underfloor heating is often best planned before the new floor is installed. Especially in an existing home, the order of work matters: first assess what is technically possible, then choose the final floor finish.

There are roughly three ways to install underfloor heating:

  • Milling into the screed: suitable when the existing screed is strong and thick enough.
  • Low-build system: useful when cutting or breaking work must stay limited.
  • New screed: often the best option for larger renovations or when insulation is added at the same time.

The right choice depends on the existing floor, insulation, floor height, doors, skirting boards and connections. A short inspection prevents surprises during the work.

Pay attention to the floor finish

Not every floor finish works equally well with underfloor heating. Tiles and natural stone transfer heat quickly. PVC and suitable engineered parquet can also work well, provided the product is approved for underfloor heating. Thick carpets or unsuitable wooden floors block heat instead.

Always check in advance whether the floor finish is compatible with underfloor heating. Also pay attention to the maximum surface temperature and thermal resistance of the product. A beautiful floor that blocks heat ultimately means less comfort and higher heating costs.

When professional advice makes sense

Professional advice is especially useful when you want to combine underfloor heating with renovation, a heat pump, a new floor finish or better insulation. Then it is not just about laying pipes, but about the whole system: heat loss, floor build-up, controls and finishing.

Advice also helps when you have complaints such as cold zones, slow warm-up or uneven heat. Often the solution is smaller than expected, but you need to know where to look.

Closing

Autumn is the right time to look seriously at underfloor heating. A short check helps prevent problems during cold days, and renovation plans can be combined smartly with floor work and insulation. That way, the heating season starts comfortably, efficiently and without unnecessary stress.
